## KPK-01: Definition and Epidemiology of Obesity

### Core Definition
Childhood and adolescent obesity is a nutritional disorder caused by long-term energy intake exceeding consumption, resulting in excessive fat accumulation and body weight exceeding 20% or more above the average standard weight for height.

### Epidemiological Data (China, 2020)
- Overweight and obesity rate among children under 6: **10.4%**
- Overweight and obesity rate among children 6~17 years: **19.0%**
- Boys have higher prevalence than girls; younger children show increasing trend
- Developing countries show rapidly rising rates, potentially surpassing developed countries

### Health Consequences
Obesity increases risk of asthma, diabetes, hypertension, acanthosis nigricans, psychological disorders, and cardiovascular diseases, with effects persisting into adulthood.

### Key Terms
- **Overweight**: A pre-obesity state with excessive fat accumulation that may cause health damage
- **Obesity (Simple obesity)**: Body weight ≥20% above average standard, caused by multiple factors
- **High-risk children**: Those with family history, unhealthy lifestyle, or overweight not yet reaching obesity

### Sources
- Report on Chinese Resident Nutrition and Chronic Disease Status (2020)

## KPK-02: Risk Factor Screening

### Three Major Risk Factors

**1. Genetic Factors**
- Genetic factors account for **20%~40%** of obesity causes
- Parental obesity is closely related to childhood obesity
- Genetic susceptibility requires environmental and behavioral factors to manifest

**2. Lifestyle Factors**
- **Dietary risks**: Skipping breakfast, excessive dinner calories, high-fat diet, binge eating, frequent sugary drinks and fast food
- **Insufficient physical activity**: Too little exercise time
- **Excessive screen time**: TV, video games
- **Inadequate sleep**: Late or insufficient sleep

**3. Psychological Factors**
- Psychological stress and negative emotional responses are important risk factors
- Hypothalamic dysfunction can disrupt appetite regulation
- Mental stress can stimulate vagus nerve activity, increasing insulin secretion and appetite

### Sources
- Jebeile H, et al. Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol, 2022

## KPK-03: TCM Etiology and Pathogenesis

### Four Major Causes
1. **Improper diet**: Excessive rich, fatty, and sweet foods damage spleen Qi
2. **Congenital constitution**: Spleen-kidney deficiency leads to Qi transformation dysfunction
3. **Sedentary lifestyle**: Excessive inactivity damages the body's Qi
4. **Emotional dysregulation**: Liver Qi stagnation disrupts Qi movement

### Classical Reference
*Suwen · Tongping Xushi Lun*: "The wealthy and noble get diseases of rich foods."

### Core Pathogenesis
**Spleen-stomach transportation dysfunction → phlegm-dampness and fatty deposits accumulate internally**

- Children inherently have weak spleen + improper diet → spleen Qi damage → phlegm-dampness generates → stomach strong/spleen weak → ravenous hunger → more eating, more deficiency
- Sedentary + congenital deficiency → spleen-kidney deficiency → water-dampness not transported → phlegm accumulates → fatty deposits form
- Emotional frustration + liver depression → liver overacts on spleen → fluid metabolism disrupted → phlegm forms → obesity

### Disease Characteristics
- **Affected organs**: Primarily spleen and stomach, involving liver and kidneys
- **Nature**: Root deficiency with branch excess (organ weakness and fluid dysfunction as root, phlegm-dampness and fatty deposits as branch)

Constitution is the特质 formed through both先天 inheritance and后天acquisition, determining susceptibility to certain diseases. Accurate constitution identification helps early obesity risk recognition and enables individualized intervention plans.

## KPK-05: Diagnostic Criteria

### Western Medicine Diagnosis
**BMI = weight(kg) / height²(m²)**

| Age | Reference | Overweight | Obese | Severely Obese |
|-----|-----------|-----------|-------|----------------|
| 0~5 years | WS/T 423 | +1SD≤BMI<+2SD | +2SD≤BMI<+3SD | BMI≥+3SD |
| 6~18 years | WS/T 586 | Sex-age specific BMI cutoff points | Same | — |

### TCM Syndrome Types (4 types)

**1. 脾虚湿阻证 (Spleen Deficiency with Dampness Encumbrance)**
- Symptoms: Obesity, fatigue, pale complexion, heavy limbs, abdominal fullness,黏腻 stool
- Tongue/pulse: Pale red, enlarged with tooth marks, white greasy coating, deep slow pulse

**2. 胃热湿阻证 (Stomach Heat with Dampness Encumbrance)**
- Symptoms: Obese and bloated, heavy limbs, ravenous hunger, bad breath, constipation or loose stool
- Tongue/pulse: Red tongue, yellow greasy coating, slippery rapid pulse

**3. 脾肾两虚证 (Spleen-Kidney Deficiency)**
- Symptoms: Puffy obesity, breathless on exertion, fatigue, sore lower back, cold limbs, lethargy, thin stool
- Tongue/pulse: Pale red, white coating, deep slow weak pulse

**4. 肝郁脾虚证 (Liver Depression with Spleen Deficiency)**
- Symptoms: Obesity, chest and rib fullness, irritability, epigastric fullness, insomnia
- Tongue/pulse: Dark red tongue, thin string-like pulse

## KPK-06: Overview of 4 Preventive Stages

### Core Concept
"Preventive treatment of disease" (治未病) is the core of TCM preventive medicine, divided into four stages:

```
Pre-disease → Sprout intervention → Disease control → Relapse prevention
(Prevent onset) (Treat at萌芽) (Stop progression) (Prevent rebound)
```

### Stage Comparison

| Stage | Target Population | Goal | Core Measures |
|-------|-----------------|------|--------------|
| 未病先防 (Pre-disease) | Normal weight with risk factors | Reduce incidence | Diet + exercise + lifestyle |
| 欲病救萌 (Sprout) | Overweight not yet obese | Treat at initial stage | Constitution-based regulation |
| 既病防变 (Disease control) | Diagnosed obesity | Lower BMI/waist/body fat | Herbal + external + diet/exercise |
| 瘥后防复 (Relapse prevention) | After successful weight loss | Long-term stability | Continue healthy habits |

### Innovation
This guideline innovatively adds the "sprout intervention" stage, expanding the traditional 3-stage framework to 4 stages, better fitting the progression of childhood obesity.
